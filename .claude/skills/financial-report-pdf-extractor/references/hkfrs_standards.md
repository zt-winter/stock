# Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (HKFRS)

## Overview

Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (HKFRS) are accounting standards issued by the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA). HKFRS is substantially converged with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), with some modifications for local requirements.

## Key HKFRS Standards for Financial Statements

### HKFRS 1: First-time Adoption of Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards
- Applies when an entity first adopts HKFRS
- Provides guidance on transition from previous GAAP

### HKAS 1: Presentation of Financial Statements
- **Structure requirements**: Specifies minimum line items for financial statements
- **Statement of financial position (balance sheet)**: Must include specific items like property, plant and equipment; investment property; intangible assets; financial assets; etc.
- **Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income**: Revenue, finance costs, tax, profit/loss
- **Statement of changes in equity**: Share capital, reserves, dividends
- **Statement of cash flows**: Operating, investing, financing activities
- **Notes**: Accounting policies, supporting information

### HKAS 2: Inventories
- Measurement at lower of cost and net realizable value
- Cost formulas (FIFO, weighted average)

### HKAS 16: Property, Plant and Equipment
- Recognition: Cost includes purchase price and directly attributable costs
- Measurement: Cost model or revaluation model
- Depreciation: Over useful life

### HKAS 17 / HKFRS 16: Leases
- HKAS 17 (older): Operating vs finance leases
- HKFRS 16 (new): Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities for most leases

### HKAS 18 / HKFRS 15: Revenue
- HKAS 18 (older): Revenue recognition criteria
- HKFRS 15 (new): Five-step model for revenue recognition

### HKAS 36: Impairment of Assets
- Impairment testing when indicators exist
- Recoverable amount: Higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use

### HKAS 37: Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets
- Recognition of provisions when obligation exists
- Contingent liabilities: Possible obligations, not recognized but disclosed

### HKAS 38: Intangible Assets
- Recognition criteria: Identifiable, controlled, future economic benefits
- Research vs development costs

### HKFRS 9: Financial Instruments
- Classification: Amortized cost, fair value through OCI, fair value through profit or loss
- Impairment: Expected credit loss model

### HKFRS 10: Consolidated Financial Statements
- Control concept for consolidation
- Requirements for consolidated statements

### HKFRS 13: Fair Value Measurement
- Fair value hierarchy: Level 1 (quoted prices), Level 2 (observable inputs), Level 3 (unobservable inputs)

## HKFRS Financial Statement Structure

### Required Statements
1. **Statement of financial position** (balance sheet)
2. **Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income**
3. **Statement of changes in equity**
4. **Statement of cash flows**
5. **Notes** including accounting policies

## HKFRS vs IFRS Differences

While HKFRS is substantially converged with IFRS, some differences exist:

1. **Small and Medium-sized Entity Framework**: HKFRS has specific SME standards
2. **Certain transitional provisions**: May differ in effective dates
3. **Industry-specific guidance**: Some Hong Kong-specific industry guidance
4. **Terminology**: Uses "statement of financial position" instead of "balance sheet" more consistently

## Practical Implications for PDF Extraction

When extracting financial statements from HKFRS-compliant reports:

1. **Look for HKFRS terminology**: "Statement of financial position" not "balance sheet"
2. **Check for Hong Kong-specific disclosures**: May reference HKICPA or Hong Kong Companies Ordinance
3. **Note bilingual presentation**: English and Chinese versions often side-by-side
4. **Currency**: Typically HKD or RMB for Hong Kong/China companies
5. **Auditor**: Usually one of the Big 4 (PwC, KPMG, Deloitte, EY) with Hong Kong office
